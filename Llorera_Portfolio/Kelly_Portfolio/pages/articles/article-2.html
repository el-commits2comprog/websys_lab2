A Slow Sunday Ritual

January 2024 · Lifestyle · 6 min read

Lifestyle Routine Mindfulness
January 28, 2024
Lifestyle
6 minutes

In a world that moves at lightning speed, I've made it my mission to protect my Sundays. Not the entire day—I still have obligations and errands like anyone else—but there's a sacred window, usually from 8 AM to noon, where I practice the art of slowing down.

Building the Perfect Sunday Morning

It starts with intention. I set my alarm for 7 AM, but there's no rush. I take my time getting out of bed, stretching, and opening the windows to let in fresh air. The first thing I do—before checking my phone—is make a cup of tea. Not coffee, though I love it. Tea feels more intentional, somehow.

I settle into an old armchair by the window with my journal, a pen, and a warm mug. For the first hour, it's just me and my thoughts. Sometimes I write, sometimes I sketch, sometimes I simply sit and observe the world waking up outside my window.

The Power of Unstructured Time

Our lives are so scheduled, so optimized. We've turned leisure into another productivity metric. But there's something revolutionary about doing absolutely nothing with intention. Not scrolling, not planning, not "self-improving"—just being.

"Rest when you're weary. Refresh and renew yourself, your body, your mind, your spirit. Then get back to work." — Ralph Marston

After journaling, I might take a long walk, visit a café I've never been to, or spend more time with books and tea. The key is that I'm not racing. I'm not checking items off a to-do list. I'm simply living.

Why This Matters

These slow Sunday mornings have become non-negotiable for my mental health. They remind me who I am beneath the productivity, the deadlines, and the endless digital noise. They ground me.

If you're looking to build your own slow ritual, start small. Even 30 minutes of intentional rest can shift your entire week. The Sundays that begin slowly ripple through the rest of my days with more patience, more presence, and more joy.
