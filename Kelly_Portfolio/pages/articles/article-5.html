It Ends So It Can Begin Again

February 2026 · New Beginnings · 8 min read

Release Endings Temporary
February 24, 2026
New Beginnings
8 minutes

There is a particular kind of grief that catches you off guard. Not the kind that announces itself, the kind you have time to prepare for. But the quiet kind — the kind that arrives only after something is already gone, when you finally turn around and realize you never got to say a proper goodbye. You didn't know, at the time, that it was the last time.

I am still learning how to hold that. How to grieve something I didn't know I loved until I no longer had it. How to let go of something I never officially held on to. It is strange work, this kind of letting go — and I am nowhere near finished with it.

The Magic of Temporary Things

I have been thinking about sunsets lately. Not any particular one, but the way they work — how the whole sky commits to something breathtaking for maybe fifteen minutes, and then it's over. You can't pause it. You can't ask it to stay a little longer. All you can do is watch, and be glad you looked up in time. In Japan, there is a word for it: mono no aware, the bittersweet awareness of impermanence. The sunset is not beautiful in spite of the fact that it fades. It is beautiful because of it. The fading is the whole point.

Some things in life are like that. They arrive without warning, bloom quietly for a season, and then — before you've had the chance to fully appreciate what you're witnessing — they're gone. And you are left standing there, in the aftermath of something you didn't know was precious until it became a memory.

I think that's what makes certain seasons of life feel so tender in hindsight. At the time, they were just ordinary. Just Tuesday afternoons and familiar voices and things you assumed would still be there tomorrow. You didn't think to hold them more carefully because you didn't know you were already in the middle of something you would one day miss.

Loving Something Without Knowing It

There are things that have shaped me that I never formally acknowledged while they were happening. People who were so woven into the texture of my days that I stopped noticing them the way you stop noticing a song you've heard too many times — until it stops playing, and the silence is suddenly enormous.

I have loved things without realizing it. I have been held and shaped and quietly changed by moments I wasn't paying enough attention to at the time. And the hardest part of losing them isn't the loss itself — it's the realization that I could have been more present. That I could have looked up a little more. Held on a little longer. Said thank you when I still had the chance.

But I am also learning to be gentler about this. You cannot appreciate everything at full volume all the time. Sometimes you only understand the value of a season once you are standing on the other side of it. That is not negligence. That is just what it means to be human and moving through time.

Unplanned Goodbyes

The goodbyes I didn't plan for are the ones that stay with me longest. The endings that arrived without ceremony, without a final conversation, without a moment where I could have gathered myself and said — this mattered, you mattered, I want you to know that before we go.

It is hard to let go of something you didn't get to finish. Something that still had loose ends, open questions, things left unsaid. There is a kind of grief in the incompleteness of it — the feeling that the story ended mid-sentence, and you are left holding the rest of the paragraph with nowhere to put it.

But I am starting to believe that some things are not meant to be finished. That the incompleteness is part of it. That certain people and places and seasons of your life are not supposed to resolve cleanly — they are supposed to linger. To keep asking questions. To stay in you long after they've left the room.

Some things are not meant to stay. But the way they shaped you — that stays forever. And maybe that's the most beautiful kind of permanence there is.

The Beauty of Endings

I used to think that a good ending was one where nothing had to end at all. Where everything you loved stayed, and the things that mattered were protected from time and change and circumstance. I thought permanence was the goal.

I don't think that anymore. The most beautiful endings I have known — the ones that actually feel like something rather than just stopping — are the ones that carry the full weight of what they're closing. The sunset is beautiful precisely because it is ending. The last chapter hits differently because it is the last. The goodbye that makes you cry in the car on the way home is the one that meant something real.

Endings are not failures. They are completions. And there is a quiet dignity in something that has run its full course — that existed entirely, and then knew when to be done.

And Then You Begin Again

Here is what I keep returning to, on the other side of every ending: you begin again. Not the same way, not from the same place — but you begin. There is always a morning after even the longest night. There is always a first step into something new, even when your legs are tired from everything that came before.

I am someone who is still learning this. Still practicing the art of releasing what has already gone, of opening my hands instead of clenching them. It does not come naturally to me. I am the kind of person who holds on — who would rather carry the weight of something than put it down and walk away. But I am learning that letting go is not the same as forgetting. That you can release something and still keep everything it gave you.

What has shaped you does not leave when it goes. It stays in the way you move through the world, the things you notice, the person you have quietly become. Every ending has already done its work in you before it closes. The leaving is just the last thing it does — and it does not undo any of the rest.

So let it go. Let it have been what it was. Let the beauty of it be that it was there at all — and that you were, too, even if you didn't fully know it at the time. It ends. And then, when you are ready, you begin again.
