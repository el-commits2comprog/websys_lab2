All Is Well — and This Day Proved It

February 2026 · Friendship · 6 min read

Gratitude Friendship Mindfulness
February 21, 2026
Friendship
6 minutes

Some days are just meant to be kept. Not photographed perfectly or written about eloquently — just held close, turned over in your hands like something precious. This was one of those days. It started with a school requirement and ended up being one of the most quietly beautiful days I've had in a long time.

A Task That Became Something More

Our Understand the Self subject assigned us to watch 3 Idiots, the 2009 Bollywood film that has somehow made its way into the hearts of millions — and now, into ours. At first it felt like just another school task to tick off the list. But my friends and I decided to make a day of it. We hanged out, bought groceries, cooked a meal, and watched it all together. What started as a requirement quietly turned into one of those afternoons I know I'll still be thinking about years from now.

We wandered through the grocery store picking out ingredients, debating what to cook, laughing at nothing in particular. There is something so ordinary and so wonderful about doing errands with people you love. By the time we got back and started cooking, the kitchen was loud and warm and full — and I remember thinking, this is it. This is the good stuff.

The Food, the Chaos, the Comfort

We cooked together, which meant it was messy and slightly chaotic and absolutely worth it. Everyone had an opinion on how things should be done. Someone almost burned something (it was me). We laughed more than we stirred. But when we finally sat down to eat what we made with our own hands, surrounded by each other — it tasted like something no restaurant could replicate. Not because the food was extraordinary, but because the company was.

There is a particular kind of ease that only exists with the right people. No performance, no pressure — just people who genuinely enjoy being in the same room together. I felt it all afternoon, and I felt grateful for it.

Three Idiots and a Mirror

Then we watched the movie. And I was not prepared for how much it would hit.

3 Idiots follows three college friends — Rancho, Farhan, and Raju — navigating the pressures of engineering school, family expectations, and the quiet terror of not knowing who you really are or what you actually want. It is funny, chaotic, and warm in all the right ways. But underneath all of that is a story about something deeply serious: the cost of living a life that isn't yours, and the rare gift of finding people who remind you of who you are when you forget.

Watching it with my friends made it feel personal in a way I didn't expect. We are college students too. We know what it's like to second-guess a course, to wonder if we chose right, to feel the weight of expectations sitting heavy on our shoulders. Rancho's line — "pursue excellence, and success will follow" — landed differently in that room, surrounded by people I've seen struggle and push through and keep going anyway.

"All is well." — 3 Idiots (2009)

The film's recurring mantra, Aal Izz Well, is not about blind optimism. It's about having the courage to calm yourself down long enough to face whatever is in front of you. It's something you say not because everything is fine, but because you need to believe it long enough to keep moving. Watching that scene, I felt something settle in my chest. We have all been in that place — scared, unsure, whispering something to ourselves just to get through. And somehow, being reminded of that alongside my friends made it feel less lonely.

Genuine Friendships and Finding Your Passion

At its heart, 3 Idiots is a love letter to genuine friendship — the kind that doesn't require you to be polished or certain or put-together. Rancho, Farhan, and Raju are so different from each other, and yet they show up for one another completely. They push each other toward honesty. They make space for each other's fears. And they celebrate each other's wins like they are their own.

I looked around at my friends while the credits rolled and thought — I have that. Maybe not perfectly, maybe not always, but I have people who know me and choose to stay anyway. That is not something to take lightly.

The movie also asks a question that sat with me long after it ended: are you pursuing what you love, or what you think you're supposed to love? For those of us still figuring it out — still finding our footing in courses and careers and callings — it felt less like a film and more like a conversation we needed to have with ourselves.

What This Day Left Me With

By the time we wrapped up, the evening had settled in quietly around us. We were full — from the food, from the laughter, from two hours of a film that somehow knew exactly what we needed to hear. It was one of those days that asks nothing of you and gives you everything anyway.

I think that's what the best days look like. Not grand or perfectly planned — just present. Just real. A grocery run, a shared meal, a movie that makes you feel understood, and the right people beside you while it happens. All is well. And this day proved it.
